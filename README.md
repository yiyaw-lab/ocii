# OCII

Open Cognitive Integrity Infrastructure for evaluating and strengthening genuine human understanding in the AI era.

---

## The Problem

As AI systems increasingly generate fluent outputs, it becomes harder to distinguish genuine understanding from surface-level performance.

OCII explores how cognition itself can become measurable, inspectable, benchmarkable, and longitudinally trackable.

---

## Core Questions

OCII investigates questions such as:

- What evidence actually indicates understanding?
- How do we distinguish recall from recognition?
- Can transfer capability be evaluated computationally?
- How should metacognitive calibration be measured?
- How can cognitive integrity be preserved in AI-mediated environments?

## Cognitive Dimensions

OCII evaluates understanding across seven dimensions:

- **Conceptual Accuracy** — whether the core idea is correctly represented
- **Mechanistic Reasoning** — whether the learner explains how or why, not just what
- **Abstraction and Compression** — whether the learner can generalize beyond the original wording
- **Transfer Capability** — whether the concept can be applied in a novel context
- **Retrieval Robustness** — whether the learner reconstructs rather than recognizes
- **Metacognitive Calibration** — whether confidence aligns with demonstrated understanding
- **Epistemic Awareness** — whether the learner understands the boundaries and assumptions of the concept

Dimension scores are generated by an LLM. The overall score is computed deterministically via weighted aggregation. This separation is intentional: it limits evaluator inconsistency and prevents hallucinated percentages.

---

## Benchmarking

OCII includes a benchmark system for evaluating the evaluator itself.

Benchmarks currently test:

- Expected score ranges per case
- Expected strong and weak dimensions
- Evaluator pass/fail consistency
- Evaluation latency

The benchmark runner is available at `/api/benchmarks` and returns a top-level `allPassed` field alongside per-case results.

---

## Current Limitations

- Evaluator latency remains high (~10–30s depending on mode)
- Benchmark coverage is limited to a small case set
- Evaluator variance across runs is still under investigation
- Scoring calibration is experimental
- Quick and full mode benchmark expectations are not yet separately defined

## Research Directions

Future research directions include:

- Evaluator consistency modeling and variance analysis
- Longitudinal cognition tracking across sessions
- Transfer graph systems linking concepts by structural similarity
- Ensemble evaluators for stability
- Retrieval robustness measurement over time
- Cognitive dependency detection

---

## Philosophy

OCII is not designed to optimize content consumption.

It is designed to explore whether genuine human understanding can remain visible, measurable, and strengthenable in the age of generative AI.
